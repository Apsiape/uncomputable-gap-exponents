\section{A final-time broadcast to three-dimensional bulk counters}
\label{sec:bulk-three}
The film separates the read length from the available computation time,
but its readers lie on an edge. We now retain the unbounded classical
computation and distribute its final word to an extensive set of finite
quantum readers. All coordinates below are periodic, with
$T_L=(\Z/L\Z)^3$ and $L=n!$. There is no external boundary term,
designated site or size-dependent interaction. The positive-coupling
statements in this section concern only
\[
 0<g<\bar g=g_5=\frac{11}{480}.
\]
Thus all selected ruler pitches satisfy $p\geq6$. The Hamiltonian
itself is defined for every real $g$.

Fix a set $A\leq_T0'$ containing $0,1,2$, an integer $\beta\geq1$,
and a total computable approximation $a(j,s)\to A(j)$.
Use an ordinary one-sided-tape machine with separate work and persistent
output tracks. At stage $s$ it computes and writes $a(j,s)$ for all
$j\leq s$. Every stage finishes. For each fixed $j$, its physical output
$o_t(j)$ is consequently constant and equal to $A(j)$ after some finite
time $t_j$. Blank output means zero. A later write to an already settled
cell has the same value. This does not require any bound on $t_j$.

\subsection{An internally created frame and a protected ruler}
We use one precise classical tiling import. The periodic-to-open
construction of Cubitt, P\'erez-Garc\'ia and Wolf
\cite[Section~6.3, equation~(133), Lemma~55]{spectralFull}, in the
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.04573v5}{version~5 full text}, is an
integer-weight nearest-neighbour layer on a square torus with minimum
energy four. Every minimizer has a single frame row and frame column,
whose complement is a valid modified Robinson tiling of an open
$(L-1)\times(L-1)$ square. Every other configuration costs at least
five. We import this finite tile set and lemma, not a new assertion
about all periodic tilings.

Put this layer in each $xy$ plane and copy its entire tile label on
every $z$ bond. If $F_z$ is the frame energy in a plane, then
\[
 E_{\rm frame}=\sum_z F_z+\#\{\text{failed tile copies}\}\geq4L.
\]
Equality forces common frame seams at all heights; failure of equality
costs at least one. Translation of the frame remains free. Its row and
column types define four adjacent-interior flags $X_0,X_1,Y_0,Y_1$.
For example, on an $x$ bond require
\[
 X_0(\text{right})=
 \ind_{\{\text{left in frame column, right outside frame column}\}}.
\]
The opposite-side test defines $X_1$, and $y$ bonds define $Y_0,Y_1$.
Each false flag has a unit diagonal penalty. These are tests on actual
torus bonds. The frame sites are tensor factors of the physical system,
not deleted sites; only the computation rules are disabled across the
marked internal edges.

In every $xz$ plane put the five-symbol ruler of
Section~\ref{sec:ruler}, with its displayed horizontal pairs along $x$
and vertical pairs along increasing $z$. Add unit $y$-bond penalties
for unequal ruler symbols. Its perfect pitch-$p$ density is
$f_g(p)=-g/p+r/p^2$, $r=1/16$. Write
\[
 f_* =\min(0,\min_{q\geq1}f_g(q)),\qquad
 \mathcal P(g)=\{q\geq2:f_g(q)=f_*\}.
\]
In the stated interval $\mathcal P(g)$ is either $\{p\}$ or, at
$g=g_p$, $\{p,p+1\}$, with $g_p$ as in
\eqref{eq:ruler-energy}. The auxiliary $q=1$ term is useful for
defective segments and never minimizes here.

For completeness, the exact charging identity on one square layer is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bulk-defect-identity}
 E-L^2f_*=
 \sum_s p_s(f_g(p_s)-f_*)
 +r\sum_s(v_s-1/p_s)+V_{\rm err}-A_0f_*.
\end{equation}
Here segments start at a $W$, $v_s$ indicates an ending $VW$ bond,
$V_{\rm err}$ counts forbidden pairs, and $A_0$ is the area of
$W$-free columns. In particular $-A_0f_*$ is retained in the equality.
It is nonnegative and may then be dropped for a lower bound.
The segment/run proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:charging} applies literally:
a vertically bad segment has deficit at least $-1$, a good open run
has deficit greater than $-1$, and a closing run has zero deficit.
A vertical violation receives at most three bad-segment and three
run-end charges, while a horizontal violation receives at most three
run-end charges. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bulk-defect-bound}
 E-L^2f_*\geq
 \sum_s p_s(f_g(p_s)-f_*)+\frac58 V_{\rm err}.
\end{equation}
This controls a putative extensive gain from defects, without a
small-perturbation estimate in the volume.

Define the nonoptimal stripe separation by excluding \emph{every}
minimizing pitch:
\[
 \delta(g)=\min_{q\geq2,\ q\notin\mathcal P(g)}
             q(f_g(q)-f_*)>0.
\]
Indeed $q(f_g(q)-f_*)=q|f_*|-g+r/q\to\infty$.
At rational $g$, a tested value $M$ excludes all $q$ with
$q|f_*|-g>M$, so the search is finite and effective. A sufficient
fixed-$g$ size cutoff includes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bulk-classical-cutoff}
 L\delta(g)\geq5/8,\qquad L^2|f_*|\geq5/8,
 \qquad q\mid L\quad(q\in\mathcal P(g)).
\end{equation}
The second condition excludes the uniform $W$-free vacua. Summing the
layer estimates over $y$, then adding copy penalties, leaves every
nonminimal three-dimensional ruler at least $5/8$ above its minimum.
The cutoff depends on $g$ and is not asserted uniform near zero.

Add a Boolean marker $v$ constant on $z$ and $y$ bonds, equal to the
indicator of ruler symbol $V$ on non-$W$ sites. On a $W$ site in the
frame column require $v=1$. In a clean stripe of pitch $q$ there are
$L/q$ marked columns. Constancy of $v$ makes these columns common to
all stripes and all $y$ planes, so different pitches cannot coexist
in a zero-penalty configuration. Conversely, any uniform pitch
dividing $L$ can be translated so that one of its $V$ columns is the
frame column. At a tie both uniform pitches remain attainable; their
mixtures incur at least one auxiliary penalty. The frame-column
condition is gated by $W$ and imposes no divider in a $W$-free vacuum.

\subsection{Three binary fields broadcast a periodic word}
\begin{lemma}[Nearest-neighbour broadcast]\label{lem:bulk-broadcast}
Suppose the ruler walls are $z=z_0$ modulo $p$, with $p\mid L$.
Introduce bits $B,C,D$ on every site, including frame and wall sites,
and impose
\begin{align*}
 B(x,y+1,z)&=B(x,y,z),& B(x,y,z+1)&=B(x,y,z),\\
 D(x,y,z)&=C(x-1,y,z),& C(x,y,z+1)&=D(x,y,z),\\
 C(x,y,z)&=B(x,y,z)&&\text{on a }W\text{ plane}.
\end{align*}
The solutions are in bijection with arbitrary $p$-periodic words
$b(x)$, with $B(x,y,z)=b(x)$. Each word has a unique $C,D$ filling.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The first two equalities make $B$ independent of $y,z$. Starting at a
wall, the next two give
\[
 C(x,y,z_0+t)=b(x-t),\qquad D(x,y,z_0+t)=b(x-t-1).
\]
At the next wall $C=B$ says exactly $b(x-p)=b(x)$. Conversely these
formulas fill each stripe consistently and close around the torus
because $p\mid L$. There is one free bit for each of the $p$ residues.
\end{proof}

Every equality is onsite or on one ordinary oriented bond. The apparent
diagonal shift is split into an $x$ bond and a $z$ bond by $D$.
There is no entire word stored onsite. On noncommensurate periodic
test graphs the free dimension can instead be $\gcd(L,p)$, identifying
different source residues. Thus this lemma is not an all-size copying
assertion. Factorial tori eventually accommodate both pitches at
each fixed tie.

\subsection{Two prefix masks and the final-time source}
Introduce an inert/active bit $I$, with unit equality penalties along
all three axes. Inert sites allow only ruler symbols $A,D$ and fix
all computation, mask and broadcast fields to prescribed dummy values.
The frame and its geometric flags remain. Active sites enable the
following predicates. Mixed phase bits are ordinary classical
configurations with positive penalties, not excluded sectors.

Let $F_z$ flag the non-$W$ row immediately after a $W$ row, and $T_z$
the non-$W$ row immediately before the next $W$. Let $F_x$ flag the
non-$V$ site immediately after a $V$ column. Each flag has a defining
two-site truth table. At pitch $p$, each stripe has height $h=p-1$.
Two copies of the Boolean thickness counter from
Section~\ref{sec:film} will mark
\[
 \ell(p)=\bitlength(p-2).
\]
Within its own horizontal interval initialize $b_j=f_j=0$ on $F_z$
and carry $c_0=1$ at the first site. The rules are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bulk-thickness}
 c_{j+1}=b_jc_j,\qquad
 b'_j=b_j\mathbin{\mathrm{XOR}}c_j,\qquad
 f'_j=f_j\mathbin{\mathrm{OR}}b_j.
\end{equation}
The first is an $x$-bond predicate; the latter two are $z$-bond
predicates. At stripe row $t$ the bits encode $t$ in binary and $f_j$
records whether bit $j$ was previously one. At $T_z$, $t=p-2$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bulk-prefix}
 b_j\mathbin{\mathrm{OR}}f_j
       =\ind_{\{2^j\leq p-2\}}=\ind_{\{j<\ell(p)\}}.
\end{equation}

The \emph{local} copy restarts after each $V$ column, has width
$h$, and has dummy $b,c,f$ fields on $V$ and $W$. Carry is enforced
only inside that interval; increment is enforced only between
non-$W$ rows. Copy the top indicator into $P_{\rm cell}$ along
\emph{all} $z$ bonds, including through $W$, and set it to zero in
$V$ columns. Do not make $P_{\rm cell}$ dummy on $W$: its vertical
copy is needed there. Alignment makes the repeated top indicators
agree, so it selects the first $\ell(p)$ sites of every local cell.

The \emph{global} copy restarts only at $X_0$ and spans the entire
frame interior of width $L-1$, without restarting at $V$. Initialize
it on each $F_z$; impose its carry only inside the frame interior
and its increment only inside the stripe. Copy its top indicator in
$P_{\rm global}$ within that non-$W$ stripe only, using dummy zero
on $W$ and the frame column. All other unused counting fields are
dummy. The resulting floor mask selects precisely the first
$\ell(p)$ tape indices after the frame column. Since that column
is a $V$ column, these are also local indices $j=0,\ldots,\ell(p)-1$
in the first cell. The identity holds after every translation and
coordinate wraparound. Moreover $\ell(p)<p-1$ for $p\geq6$,
leaving an actual nonreader neighbour after the last reader site.

At every stripe floor $F_z$ run the fixed machine in the internally
open $xy$ square, using $x$ as tape and $y$ as time. Its rows are
$0,\ldots,L-2$ and its width is $L-1$. The two-site arrival compiler
of Section~\ref{sec:classical} applies: horizontal predicates fix
the incoming head signals from neighbours, and vertical predicates
fix the next tape, head and persistent output from that finite onsite
tuple. Both missing-edge arrival fields are blank, including fields
in the final row; all inactive machine fields are dummy. Work and
output tracks are distinct. Initialize the head at $X_0,Y_0$.
At physical time $t$ its position is at most $t$, so an included
transition cannot require tape beyond the right edge. Functional
induction gives the ordinary infinite-tape computation on all
included rows. The divider columns do not cut this giant tableau.

Impose the onsite source equality only at
$F_z\wedge Y_1\wedge P_{\rm global}$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bulk-final-source}
 B=o_{L-2}(j),\qquad 0\leq j<\ell(p).
\end{equation}
Also impose $B=0$ wherever $P_{\rm cell}=0$. There is no equality
between $B$ and the machine output at earlier time rows. Computations
on different stripe floors have the same initialization and final
time, so their demands agree. Lemma~\ref{lem:bulk-broadcast} now
forces this final prefix, repeated in every cell throughout the
volume, with zeros on the other residues.

\begin{lemma}[Simultaneous completion]\label{lem:bulk-completion}
For each minimum frame and each aligned uniform minimum pitch dividing
$L$, the active auxiliary fields have a zero-penalty filling. Every
such filling has the same repeated radix word
$[2^{\beta o_{L-2}(j)}:j<\ell(p)]$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fill the two thickness counters functionally, copy their top masks
with their respective boundary conventions, and fill each identical
machine tableau. Set the $p$-periodic word to the final prefix and
zeros elsewhere. Its $C,D$ extension exists by the broadcast lemma.
All source, mask and alignment predicates are satisfied. Conversely,
functional counters and tableaux fix both masks and the source
prefix, and the zero mask fixes every remaining residue. The
broadcast lemma fixes $C,D$. The Robinson interior need not be
unique; it does not enter the radix predicates.
\end{proof}

All added constraints have unit nonnegative weight. Any Boolean fact
derived from a neighbour is stored onsite and checked on its defining
bond. Later predicates use only that stored fact and other onsite
fields. Hence the compiler uses a fixed finite alphabet and only
onsite or nearest-neighbour rational projectors. Neither an oracle
nor a volume-dependent register enters an interaction coefficient.

\subsection{The full Hamiltonian and its protected positive gap}
Activate the guarded counter of Section~\ref{sec:counter} on every
$x$ segment with $I=1$, $T_z=1$ and $P_{\rm cell}=1$, for every
$y$, including the internal frame row. Its first-site rule uses
$F_x$ and its radix is $2^{\beta B}$. Wrap uses the actual bond from
the final reader site to its nonreader neighbour. Freeze every
exterior dynamic field to $(0,\NL)$ with a unit penalty. Retain all
parallel local edges. On inconsistent classical labels, each
controlled transition is still a nonnegative edge Laplacian times
a classical projector. Thus every quantum block remains nonnegative.

Put a qubit at each site as well. When $I=1$, penalize its state
$|1\rangle$ by one. On a $y$ bond with both endpoints inert put
\[
 (|01\rangle-|10\rangle)(\langle01|-\langle10|),
\]
and otherwise no qubit bond term. Inert counter fields are frozen.
This completes a fixed nearest-neighbour family with rational
coefficient matrices, affine in $g$ only through the ruler bonus.

\begin{lemma}[Eventual distinct-level identity]\label{lem:bulk-gap}
For each fixed $0<g<\bar g$, for all sufficiently large $L=n!$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bulk-gap-identity}
 \Delta(L,g)=\min_{q\in\mathcal P(g)}d_q,\qquad
 d_q=\Delta^+\bigl(K[2^{\beta A(j)}:j<\ell(q)]\bigr).
\end{equation}
The threshold is finite but has no general computable bound.
For $g\leq0$, $\lim_n\Delta(n!,g)=0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
All classical fields are diagonal and unchanged by quantum moves, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bulk-blocks}
 H_L(g)=\bigoplus_\sigma
       \bigl(E_{\rm cl}(\sigma;g)\ind+K_\sigma\bigr),
 \qquad K_\sigma\geq0.
\end{equation}
The frame and ruler lower bounds, together with zero auxiliary
penalties, are simultaneously attained by
Lemma~\ref{lem:bulk-completion}. Quantum kernels are nonempty.
Consequently the ground energy is exactly
\[
 E_*(L,g)=4L+L^3f_*.
\]
After \eqref{eq:bulk-classical-cutoff}, any nonminimum frame costs
at least one and any nonminimum ruler costs at least $5/8$.
A failed flag, tableau, copy, alignment or phase equality costs an
integer at least one. The bare ruler never lies below its own
minimum, so a changed phase cannot cancel such a penalty. An all-inert
configuration cannot attain $f_*<0$. Every nonminimizing classical
block therefore starts at least $5/8$ above $E_*$. Nonnegativity of
$K_\sigma$ excludes even an arbitrarily long fake reader in those
blocks; superposition across diagonal blocks cannot lower an offset.

In a minimum block of uniform pitch $q$, the number of independent
identical readers is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bulk-reader-count}
 N_L=(L/q)(L/q)L=L^3/q^2.
\end{equation}
The factors count stripes in $z$, intervals in $x$, and all $y$ rows.
Other quantum factors have unit gaps. The first positive eigenvalue
of a tensor sum with nonempty factor kernels is the least positive
factor gap, even when the kernels are degenerate.

Set $T(q)=\max_{j<\ell(q)}t_j$. For $L-2\geq T(q)$ the finite
radix word is exactly the one defining $d_q$. There are only one or
two pitches to settle. With
$k_q=\beta|A\cap[0,\ell(q))|$, Lemma~\ref{lem:counter} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bulk-counter-bracket}
 \frac{4^{-k_q}}{64\ell(q)^2}\leq d_q
 \leq 4\pi^2\,4^{-k_q}\leq\frac{\pi^2}{16}<\frac58.
\end{equation}
The last step uses $q\geq6$, $\beta\geq1$ and $0,1,2\in A$.
Thus the full ground-space gap is supplied by a reader. At a tie
alignment retains the two uniform classical minima and removes their
mixtures. Taking the least positive energy over both ground blocks
gives $\min(d_p,d_{p+1})$, as claimed. No equality between $d_q$ and
the main counter cycle is used: closed free paths, zero-head states,
penalized components and multiple-head sectors are all covered by
Lemma~\ref{lem:counter}. The legal path counterexample in
Section~\ref{sec:film} still applies.

For $g\leq0$ the bare ruler minimum is zero, attained by a uniform
$A$ or $D$ vacuum. With $I=0$ and dummy auxiliaries the frame minimum
$4L$ is attained, and all quantum terms are nonnegative. The inert
qubit layer has the all-zero ground vector. On one of its $y$ rings
the single-excitation Fourier vector has positive eigenvalue
\[
 \epsilon_L=2-2\cos(2\pi/L)\quad(L\geq3).
\]
Therefore $0\leq\Delta(L,g)\leq\epsilon_L\to0$. Only this positive
upper witness is needed, without an assertion about the ring's full
gap or a unique ground vector.
\end{proof}

The machine settling time in this proof need not be computably bounded
as $q$ varies. The geometric cutoff is effective at rational $g$;
the further settling cutoff is not. The volume limit is taken at
fixed $g$ before the pitch grows. In particular it is not an infimum
over the machine's intermediate guesses.

\subsection{The critical law and every prescribed third-level value}
The logarithm of \eqref{eq:bulk-counter-bracket} yields
\[
 2k_p-\log_2(4\pi^2)\leq\log_2(1/d_p)
            \leq2k_p+6+2\log_2\ell(p).
\]
Since $\ell(p)=\log_2p+O(1)$ and $p=2r/g+O(1)$, off ties,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bulk-density-exponent}
 \frac{\log(1/\Delta_\infty(g))}{\log(1/g)}
 =2\beta\rho_{\ell(p)}(A)
      +O\!\left(\frac{\log\ell(p)}{\ell(p)}\right).
\end{equation}
Every sufficiently large pitch, and every sufficiently large
bitlength, is visited. At a tie the inverse logarithm is the larger
of the neighbouring values. Both have the same density limit and
vanishing error, including when the bitlength increases. Thus if
$A$ has density $\rho$, the exponent is $2\beta\rho$.

Here is the needed density realization explicitly. For a finite
$\alpha\geq0$ computable in $0''$, choose a fixed integer
$\beta\geq\max(1,\alpha/2)$ and put $\rho=\alpha/(2\beta)$.
The relativized limit lemma supplies a $0'$-computable rational
sequence $u_s\in[0,1]$ tending to $\rho$. Starting with $N_0=1$,
put $N_{s+1}=(s+2)N_s$ and on $[N_s,N_{s+1})$ use the mechanical
word of slope $u_s$: its first $t$ bits have sum $\lfloor tu_s\rfloor$.
For an intermediate prefix the density differs by at most
$1/(N_s+t)$ from
\[
 \frac{N_s\rho_{N_s}(A)+tu_s}{N_s+t}.
\]
At the endpoint its difference from $u_s$ is at most
$1/(s+2)+1/N_{s+1}$. Endpoints converge to $\rho$, and the displayed
convex combination then proves convergence of all intermediate
densities. The resulting set is computable in $0'$; changing its
first three bits to one preserves the density. The ordinary limit
lemma gives the total computable approximation used by the machine.
The ordinary algorithm for that approximation, rather than an oracle,
is encoded in the finite local alphabet. Equation~\eqref{eq:bulk-density-exponent}
proves the required exponent $\alpha$.

The gap closes even for $\alpha=0$. The orbit in
\eqref{eq:orbit} has length at least $2\ell$, so its upper witness
gives $d_p\leq\pi^2/\ell(p)^2\to0$. The ground energy per volume is
zero on $g\leq0$, and for small $g>0$ it is $f_*$. Indeed the
frame contribution is $4/L^2\to0$, and completing the square gives
\[
 -\frac gp+\frac r{p^2}
 =-\frac{g^2}{4r}+r\left(\frac1p-\frac g{2r}\right)^2.
\]
Rounding $p$ near $2r/g$ makes the last term $O(g^4)$.
The two different one-sided quadratic behaviors preclude real
analyticity at the computable point zero. Together with the gapless
inert side and the positive gaps closing from the right, this proves
the critical-point assertion.

For necessity, at rational $g$ a finite torus matrix is rational.
Multiplicity-aware algebraic root isolation computes its two lowest
distinct eigenvalues whenever defined. Replace their gap by rational
approximants with effective errors tending to zero. For an existing
inner limit a halting oracle finds tail-Cauchy indices, giving a
uniform $0'$-Cauchy name. Promised positivity allows a search for a
certified interval bounded away from zero in that same oracle, and
then computation of the logarithm. One further existing coupling
limit is computable in $0''$, as in
Proposition~\ref{prop:critical-ceiling}. A computable critical point
can be approached by certified rational points on its required side
without adding an oracle. No decidability of strict rational cuts of
arbitrary computable real arrays is assumed.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:bulk-beyond-sides}
Some fixed three-dimensional family in Theorem~\ref{thm:bulk-three}
has an existing positive gap exponent outside
$\mathsf S_2\cup\mathsf P_2$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Enumerate all $\Sigma^0_2$ and $\Pi^0_2$ subsets of $\Q$, including
noncuts. Oracle $0''$ decides membership of a chosen midpoint in
each candidate. Starting inside $[1/4,3/4]$, choose the left third
of the current interval when its midpoint belongs to the candidate,
and the right third otherwise. If the candidate is a strict lower
cut, its real lies above the midpoint in the first case and at or
below it in the second, so the chosen third excludes that real.
The nested intervals have a unique $0''$-computable point, different
from every listed cut-real. Apply the attainment just proved.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Extensive local excitations and a selected infinite-volume phase}
Fix $g\in(0,\bar g)$ and a pitch $p$ attaining
\eqref{eq:bulk-gap-identity}; at a tie choose a gap-attaining one.
In its minimum classical block choose normalized counter vectors
$\phi\in\ker K$ and $K\psi=d_p\psi$. Use $\phi$ on every reader
and the prescribed states on all exterior factors, giving a product
ground vector $\Omega_L$. Replacing $\phi$ by $\psi$ at any of the
$N_L=L^3/p^2$ reader locations gives mutually orthogonal eigenvectors
at $E_*+d_p$, since $\langle\phi,\psi\rangle=0$. Their support
length $\ell(p)$ is independent of $L$ at fixed $g$. They occupy
every $y$ row and a periodic array in $xz$, including locations
arbitrarily far from the frame seams. Linear combinations give
extended modes in the same extensive flat band.

More quantitatively, let $a=|\psi\rangle\langle\phi|$ on one
reader's dynamic factors and identity on other factors. It has
norm one and finite support. If $P_d$ projects onto the first
positive energy in this classical block and $\tau_u$ denotes
spatial translation, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bulk-local-weight}
 \frac1{L^3}\sum_{u\in T_L}
       \|P_d\tau_u(a)\Omega_L\|^2\geq\frac1{p^2}.
\end{equation}
Every aligned reader translate contributes one. The bound persists
as volume tends to infinity at fixed $g$; neither its value nor the
support size is asserted uniform as $g\downarrow0$.

To obtain an infinite-volume phase, translate the common frame so
its two seams recede from every fixed window, retain a fixed ruler
phase and the settled word, and pass to a locally convergent
subsequence of the remaining finite-alphabet patterns. Choose the
same reader product vectors. Finite-range dynamics converge on
local observables uniformly on compact time intervals, by the
standard Lieb--Robinson thermodynamic-limit construction
\cite{liebRobinson}. Every finite-volume ground-state excitation
spectral measure is supported in $\{0\}\cup[d_p,\infty)$. Its
weak limit excludes $(0,d_p)$. Local vectors are dense in the
ground-state GNS representation, so its Hamiltonian has no spectrum
there. A fixed aligned reader operator retains unit spectral weight
at $d_p$ (its time correlation is the corresponding exponential).
Thus the gap in this selected representation is exactly $d_p$
above the \emph{entire} zero-energy subspace. At a tie the same
argument uses the selected gap-attaining pitch. This completes the
proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:bulk-three}.

\subsection{Gap conventions and effective selection}
\label{subsec:bulk-conventions}
The positive-density statement is not an identification with every
operator-algebraic gap definition. The locally nondegenerate criterion
of Xu et al.\ \cite[equation~(4), Definition~1.11]{bulkGap} requires
\[
 \omega(a^*[H,a])\geq
 \gamma\bigl(\omega(a^*a)-|\omega(a)|^2\bigr)
\]
and their bulk gap takes a supremum over KMS ground states satisfying
that criterion. Our readers have local zero-energy degeneracy.
Choose orthogonal $\phi_0,\phi_1\in\ker K$ and the local map
$|\phi_1\rangle\langle\phi_0|$ in the product state using $\phi_0$.
Its energy is zero and its variance one, excluding every positive
$\gamma$ in this state. Already $\operatorname{diag}(0,0,1)$ has
distinct-level gap one but zero locally nondegenerate threshold.
Neither a unique zero-energy vector nor the KMS-state supremum is
computed here. The certification theorem for that other convention
therefore supplies no contradictory ceiling for the present result.

There is also a useful sufficient effective-collapse condition.
If, at every rational $g$, a computable procedure supplies the
complete finite list of limiting reader operators with their correct
radix labels, and a competing-sector threshold above their gaps,
then it computes the limiting gap. Its existing exponent is
$\mathsf D_2$. An effective volume settling bound suffices, but is
not necessary if these operators are already known. Here the pitch
is computable; its settled word $A\upharpoonright\ell(p)$ is not
uniformly computable in general. The finite periodic quantum readers
do not provide an effective list of their limiting classical labels.

Two obstructions explain the construction choices. Retaining
independent readers for every previous computation stage would give
$\min_{s\leq s(L)}d_s$, tending to $\inf_s d_s$, a right-c.e.
quantity for computable stage gaps. The history
$1/8,1/2,1/2,\ldots$ would retain $1/8$, not the final value $1/2$.
The final-time source withdraws old guesses instead. If the union
$\mathcal A_L$ of all available reader supports were confined to
one seam, a norm-bounded local operator of radius $R$ would have
spectral weight into that factorized band at most
$\|a\|^2|\mathcal A_L^{(+R)}|/L^3$, tending to zero. The relevant
union is over spatially different reader locations, not the
degeneracy of a single reader. Equation~\eqref{eq:bulk-local-weight}
establishes the extensive union here.

Finally, coupling equicontinuity alone does not compute a volume
limit even when it is pointwise eventually exact. For the same
computable approximation let
\[
 k_{n,s}=\sum_{j<n}a(j,s),\qquad
 q_n=2^{-n},\qquad y_{n,s}=2^{-n-k_{n,s}}.
\]
On $[0,1]$, let $F_s(0)=0$, interpolate the knots
$(q_n,y_{n,s})$ for $0\leq n\leq s$, and join the last knot to
zero linearly. A knot-interval slope is
\[
 2^{-k_{n,s}}\bigl(2-2^{-a(n,s)}\bigr)\leq3/2;
\]
the terminal slope is at most one. These uniformly computable
piecewise-linear functions have a common Lipschitz constant.
At each fixed $g>0$ their values eventually settle. But the limiting
value at $q_n$ identifies $k_n$ among the finitely many separated
powers of two, and successive $k_n$ recover $A$. A computable limit
would therefore compute $A$. At the knots its normalized inverse
logarithm is $1+\rho_n(A)$. Neighbouring knot ratios lie between
two and four, so interpolation changes this by $O(1/n)$. When
the density exists the exponent is $1+\rho(A)$, potentially outside
both one-sided level-two classes. This is a scalar-data example,
not another Hamiltonian realization. Effective boundary-to-volume
brackets for energy density remain an additional argument.

\subsection{Finite checks and the exact scope of the theorem}
The ancillary programs \texttt{check\_bulk\_broadcast.py} and
\texttt{check\_bulk\_independent.py} implement separate finite checks.
The first checks literal copying components, both prefix counters,
exact ruler minimization and small-torus charging, tie alignment,
the inert-ring witness, tensor gaps and the scalar modulus example.
The second uses independent binary row elimination on translated
geometries and all tested source fillings, forces contradictions
from changed source bits, checks defective rulers and complete
native counter products, and reproduces the long legal free paths
and degenerate-kernel tensor sums. It also checks tie arithmetic,
physical endpoint guards and the reader count. The expected
noncommensurate collapse and failure of universal main-cycle
equality are included as counterexamples to those stronger assertions.

These programs do not generate the full onsite matrix for an
arbitrary target machine, diagonalize a three-dimensional many-body
Hamiltonian, reprove the imported frame lemma, or numerically certify
a noncomputable limit. Floating-point trigonometric checks are
corroboration, not interval-certified proofs. The finite-rule
compiler and block argument supply the general statements.
There is no conclusion here about all integer sizes, robustness,
unique ground states, a separate correlation length, minimum spatial
dimension, or an extremum over all infinite-volume phases. The
classical computation still uses an internal anchor. Positive-coupling
inner limits outside $(0,\bar g)$ are not claimed.
