\section{A film whose inner gap limit becomes exact}\label{sec:film}
The finite-size engine writes a fast \emph{computable} word because its
available time and read length grow together. A film separates them.
Use boxes $[0,L)^2\times[0,M)$ with coordinates $(x,y,z)$, take
$L\to\infty$ first, and only then scale $M$. The film gap and its
normalized exponent sequence are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:film-definition}
 Q(M)=\lim_{L\to\infty}\Delta(L,M)>0,\qquad
 z_M=\frac{\log(1/Q(M))}{\log M}.
\end{equation}
All intermediate gaps and limits used in a necessity assertion must be
defined. The construction below makes the inner limit eventually exact.

\subsection{The local fields and their determination}
Put two flag bits on each coordinate axis, indicating its near and far
faces. For each flag use onsite energy $-1$ when set and a bond
contribution $+2$ at the endpoint that is not on the designated face.
The six independent copies of Lemma~\ref{lem:boundary} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:film-flags}
 E_{\rm cl}(\sigma)=E_*+\#\{\text{flag errors}\}
                          +\#\{\text{rule violations}\},\qquad
 E_*=-2(L^2+2LM).
\end{equation}
This is an exact identity, including coincident opposite faces when a
side has length one. Every additional forbidden onsite or bond pattern
has unit nonnegative cost.

Let $A$ be a $\Delta^0_2$ set, with a total computable approximation
$a(j,s)\to A(j)$. Modify the first three bits to be one at every stage.
On $z=0$, a fixed ordinary machine uses $y$ as time and $x$ as tape.
It first initializes output cells $0,1,2$ permanently to one in a
known finite time. It then computes $a(j,s)$ for $j=0,\ldots,s$ at stage $s$ and writes each
answer to its own cell on a persistent output track. Work and output
are separate tracks. Each stage finishes. For each fixed $j$ there is
therefore a physical time $t_j$ after which every subsequent output at
cell $j$ equals $A(j)$. Blank output is read as zero.

The two-site arrival compiler of Section~\ref{sec:classical} specifies
this tableau. Initialize at $y=0$ with the head at $x=0$; fix both
missing-neighbour arrival fields at $x=0,L-1$. Freeze \emph{every}
machine field to one dummy value off $z=0$, and guard both endpoints
of machine bond rules by $z=0$. The head cannot travel farther than
elapsed time, so all included rows coincide with the infinite
one-sided computation. This proves existence and uniqueness without
a bound on the runtime of a stage.

In every $(x,z)$ slice put a separate binary thickness counter
$(b,c,f)$. At $z=0$ set $b=f=0$; at $x=0$ set $c=1$. Across an
$x$ bond set $c_{x+1}=b_xc_x$, and across a $z$ bond set
\[
 b'=b\mathbin{\mathrm{XOR}}c,\qquad
 f'=f\mathbin{\mathrm{OR}}b.
\]
Functional induction shows that row $z$ encodes $z$ in binary, and at
$z=M-1$ the bit $b_j\mathbin{\mathrm{OR}}f_j$ equals
$\ind_{\{2^j\leq M-1\}}$. Copy the machine's output bit along $z$
on another field, initialized at $z=0$. These recurrences determine
all counter and transport fields, including those not read by the
quantum layer.

The quantum region is
\[
 \reg=Y_1Z_1(b\mathbin{\mathrm{OR}}f),\qquad
 \ell(M)=\bitlength(M-1).
\]
For $L>\ell(M)$ it is one segment at $(y,z)=(L-1,M-1)$.
Its radices are $2^{\beta o'_j}$, where $o'$ is the transported bit.
All other quantum fields are frozen. Use exactly the guarded rules
\eqref{eq:increment}--\eqref{eq:restart}. In particular wrap tests an
$x$ bond with region on the left and nonregion on the right; it changes
only the left dynamic field. There is a physical such neighbour in
every asymptotic box. No three-site interaction or absent-neighbour
oracle is introduced. All coefficients are integers.

\begin{theorem}[Exact fixed-thickness gap]\label{thm:film}
For every $\Delta^0_2$ set $A$ containing $0,1,2$ and integer
$\beta\geq1$, the construction has, for all sufficiently large fixed
$M$ and all $L\geq L_0(M)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:film-exact}
 \Delta(L,M)=Q(M)
 =\Delta^+\bigl(K[2^{\beta A(j)}:j<\ell(M)]\bigr).
\end{equation}
Here $K$ is the full counter operator, not only its main orbit.
The threshold need not have a computable bound. Its exponent sequence
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:film-density}
 z_M=2\beta\rho_{\ell(M)}(A)
           +O\!\left(\frac{\log\log M}{\log M}\right).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Flags, counters, the machine and transport are uniquely determined in
that order. Equation~\eqref{eq:film-flags} puts all other classical
blocks at least one above this block. Their quantum operators are
nonnegative. Incorrect exterior quantum fields also cost at least one
and cannot move. The counter analysis in Lemma~\ref{lem:counter} covers
zero heads, multiple heads, penalized components, and all free paths
and cycles, retaining parallel-edge multiplicities.

At fixed $M$, choose
$L_0(M)>\max(\ell(M)+1,\max_{j<\ell(M)}t_j)$. This is finite because
only finitely many output cells are read. Above it the radix vector
is exactly the same for every $L$. For large $M$ its first three
radices are $2^\beta$, so its gap is at most $4\pi^2/64<1$.
It is therefore the gap of the entire box. Taking logarithms of
Lemma~\ref{lem:counter}, with
$k=\beta|A\cap[0,\ell(M))|$, proves \eqref{eq:film-density}.
Since $\ell(M)$ takes every large integer through unit steps, the two
endpoints are exactly $2\beta$ times the density endpoints of $A$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Film classification]\label{cor:film-classification}
For translation-invariant integer nearest-neighbour interactions on
$\Z^3$, existing nonnegative finite film exponents are exactly
$\mathsf D_3\cap[0,\infty)$. Ordered finite nonnegative pairs are
exactly $\mathsf S_3\times\mathsf P_3$. Some fixed model has an
existing exponent in neither $\mathsf S_2$ nor $\mathsf P_2$.
Necessity holds for algebraic interactions in any fixed dimension,
under the stated existence and positivity promises.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For necessity, isolate the distinct finite algebraic eigenvalues, as
in Proposition~\ref{prop:necessity}. A fixed-thickness inner limit has
a uniform $0'$-Cauchy name. Its promised positivity permits computation
of its logarithm in that oracle. Proposition~\ref{prop:calculus}
then gives the three outer ceilings. For attainment choose
$\beta\geq\max(1,b/2)$ and apply Lemma~\ref{lem:relative-density} at
level $k=2$ to $a/(2\beta),b/(2\beta)$. Theorem~\ref{thm:film} applies.
For an existing value outside both preceding sides, use the diagonal
real of Lemma~\ref{lem:diagonal} and a positive rational scaling.
\end{proof}

No uniform gap equality with the main counter cycle is asserted.
For example the legal radix vector $(2,2,2,1^{15})$ has a main cycle
of length $58$, but also a closed free path of length $30$ with gap
$2-2\cos(\pi/30)<2-2\cos(2\pi/58)$. Fix digits
$d_0=d_1=0,d_2=1$ and all later digits zero. An incrementing head
travels from position $3$ to $17$, wraps, and returns to $3$; it can
neither enter nor leave through digit $d_2=1$. Its forced side symbols
are legal throughout. The independent component check also finds a
longer free path. The full-counter bracket, exact formula
\eqref{eq:film-exact}, and density exponent do not require identifying
the minimizing component.

\subsection{A classical moving-corner observable}
The same arithmetic level occurs without a quantum layer. In a square
tableau use the limit machine above, and index a site by $(j,i)$ back
from the \emph{far-time} corner. Its physical time is $L-1-i$.
At fixed $(j,i)$ the output tends to $A(j)$ as $L\to\infty$.
The $J\times J$ average of that limiting configuration is
\[
 J^{-2}\sum_{i,j<J}A(j)=J^{-1}\sum_{j<J}A(j).
\]
Thus its existing density and ordered endpoints fill the same level-three
classes on the allowed interval. Necessity is one pointwise limit of
computable finite tableaux followed by the window-size limit. A fixed
site measured from the initialized corner would instead remain at
fixed computation time. This is a moving-corner boundary-selected
observable, not a claim about a canonical translation-invariant bulk
state density.
